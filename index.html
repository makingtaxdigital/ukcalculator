What Making Tax Digital actually changes

Right now, most self-employed people and landlords do one tax return a year, usually in a rush every January. Making Tax Digital replaces that with digital record-keeping plus a short update sent to HMRC every three months, followed by one final submission that wraps up the year — the same underlying obligation, reported more often instead of all at once.

It isn't new in general — VAT-registered businesses have worked this way for years already. What's rolling out now, in stages through 2026 to 2028, is the income tax version, aimed specifically at self-employed people and landlords whose income crosses the thresholds above.

HMRC's own reasoning: a single once-a-year return, reconstructed from memory and old receipts, produces a lot of honest mistakes. Smaller, more frequent updates are meant to catch errors sooner and give a clearer running picture. For the official rules, see gov.uk's guidance directly.

Once MTD applies, you'll need digital records

HMRC requires software that can keep digital records and send quarterly updates automatically. Xero is one of the recognised options, built to handle Making Tax Digital submissions.
