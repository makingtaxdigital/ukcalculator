Frequently asked questions

Do I need to register if I earn under £1,000?

No. HMRC's trading allowance means self-employment turnover under £1,000 in a tax year doesn't need to be reported at all. If your self-employed income is less than that, you don't need to register as self-employed, and Making Tax Digital doesn't come into it.

Once your turnover goes over £1,000, you do need to register — though registering doesn't necessarily mean you'll owe tax, since that depends on your profit after expenses and your personal allowance.

What if I have self-employed income AND rental or property income?

They count together, not separately. HMRC adds your self-employment turnover and your property income into one combined figure — called qualifying income — and checks that total against the threshold.

So someone earning £25,000 from self-employment and £10,000 from a rental property has £35,000 in qualifying income, even though neither amount alone crosses the £30,000 mark. This is one of the most commonly missed parts of the rules, which is why the calculator on this site asks about both.

What software actually counts as MTD-compliant?

It has to be software HMRC has specifically recognised as capable of keeping digital records and sending quarterly updates directly to HMRC — a spreadsheet or a general accounting app on its own doesn't qualify unless it connects to HMRC in the right way.

Well-known recognised options include QuickBooks, FreeAgent, and Coconut, among others. HMRC keeps a full, up-to-date list of recognised software on gov.uk, which is worth checking directly since new tools are added over time.

Does this apply to me if I only sell occasionally, like clearing out old clothes?

Generally no. HMRC has been clear that selling your own personal, secondhand items — clothes you no longer wear, old furniture, and similar — isn't trading, and doesn't count towards these thresholds at all. This is specifically about income from a genuine trade: buying or making things to sell for profit, or providing a service, on a regular basis.

I'm not sure my situation fits these examples — what should I do?

This calculator gives a general picture based on the rules as published, but it isn't personal advice. For anything specific to your situation, gov.uk or a qualified accountant is the right place to check.
